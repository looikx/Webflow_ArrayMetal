The stack, and what to subscribe to

A straight verdict on Webflow, the architecture that replaces it, and the exact list of services to sign up for — with what each one does, what it costs, and when you actually need to buy it.

Is Webflow suitable?

Worth answering carefully, because the honest answer changed once we decided to publish ~5,000 item codes instead of 574,609.

NO — MOVE OFF

It is no longer a capacity problem. At ~400 product pages, ~20 systems and ~5,000 published item codes, you would need roughly 5,400 CMS items across 11 collections. Webflow Premium allows 20,000 items and 40 collections — it would fit. So the old argument (“574k SKUs won’t go in a CMS”) no longer applies.

It is a capability problem. Four things you have decided you want are things Webflow cannot do natively, and three of them you already discovered yourself while building catalogue/.

Do not buy everything on day one. Each service is only needed when its phase starts.

NOW

The one thing that blocks everything else, and it is a sales question, not an engineering one: which item codes belong in the public tables? Start with AML in HDG — straights plus the common fittings.

Buy nothing. Costs a meeting.

Why this shape and not another

Why not put products in the CMS?

Sanity’s free plan caps at 10,000 documents and Growth at 25,000. Even 5,000 published codes would eat most of that for no benefit, and every dimension would become a second copy that can drift from the costing DB.

Why not build our own CMS?

You would be building rich-text editing, image cropping, draft/publish, revision history and role management — six months of work unrelated to cable ladders, and marketing would end up with a worse tool.

Why Sanity over Storyblok?

Storyblok is better at visual page-building but its realistic plan for a marketing team runs to $349/month, and its flexibility is exactly what lets an editor break a structure. Sanity’s schema lives in code, so marketing physically cannot add a field or change a type.

Why Vercel and not Railway?

Railway could host it, but a traffic spike on the public site must never slow down the tool your team prices jobs with. Separate hosts, separate blast radius — and Vercel’s caching and image handling directly affect SEO. Full comparison below.

Why not a separate Supabase project?

Another $25/month and a cross-project sync job to solve a problem a database role already solves. A dedicated web_reader with access to one schema cannot see prices. Split it later if an audit demands it — the projection makes that a connection-string change.

Why no search engine yet?

Browsing is always scoped to one system and product type — a few hundred rows. Postgres answers that in milliseconds. Add Meilisearch when search genuinely feels slow, not before.

Could we run everything on one platform?

Asked and answered — it matters, because two vendors means two bills and two dashboards.

Move the costing app to Vercel? — No

NOT POSSIBLE

The worker is a persistent process. railway.worker.toml runs new Worker(…) with restartPolicyType = "always" — it sits connected to Redis waiting for jobs, indefinitely. Vercel functions start on a request and die when they return. There is nowhere to put it.

And the jobs are long. runRecalculate pages through every item in a category — 121,231 SKUs for ART50 in a single run — appending to price_records and updating progress in job_records as it goes. Vercel's ceiling is 800 seconds generally available, 30 minutes in beta with per-function configuration. Betting your pricing pipeline on that timeout is not a trade worth making. BullMQ with maxRetriesPerRequest: null also assumes a long-lived Redis connection, which serverless churns.

You could rewrite the jobs as durable workflows or chunked cron slices. That is re-engineering a working system for no business gain, and it puts the append-only price history at risk across chunk boundaries.

Move the website to Railway instead? — Defensible

This is the better version of the question. Railway hosts Next.js perfectly well.

Recommendation stands: Vercel for the public site. Not for features — for blast radius. The costing tool is what your team prices jobs with; a crawler or a bad marketing deploy must not be able to saturate it. Separate platforms make that impossible by construction, and $20/month is cheap insurance.

But Railway + Cloudflare is a legitimate choice if you would rather run one vendor — the Singapore region genuinely helps SEA latency. If you take it, run the website as its own Railway service, never inside the costing service, so you keep process isolation.
